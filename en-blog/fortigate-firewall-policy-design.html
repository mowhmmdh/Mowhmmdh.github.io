FortiGate Firewall Policy Design: A Secure, Readable and Maintainable Rule Base

A reliable firewall policy should not only pass traffic. You should be able to explain why every rule exists, what it permits and when it should be reviewed or removed.

1. Start with the real requirement

Limit source, destination, service and time to what the business actually needs. Least privilege should be visible in the rule itself rather than left as an undocumented assumption.

2. Treat rule order as security logic

Specific rules should be evaluated before broad rules. A general allow rule near the top can make carefully designed rules below it ineffective and harder to audit.

3. Use segmentation deliberately

Users, servers, management networks, guests and sensitive services should not automatically share the same trust level. Inter-segment traffic should be an explicit and reviewable decision.

4. Separate access decisions from NAT troubleshooting

Ask two different questions: is the traffic permitted, and does the traffic require address translation? Keeping these concepts separate makes diagnosis much faster.

5. Make logging useful

Logging everything without a review plan creates noise. Prioritize sensitive rules, important denies and flows that require traceability, then retain logs long enough to support investigation.

6. Review unused and shadowed policies

Old and unused rules increase complexity and may preserve access that is no longer required. Check usage, dependencies and recent changes before removing or consolidating a rule.

Review checklist

  • Every rule has a clear purpose.
  • Sources and destinations are no broader than required.
  • Services are limited and explainable.
  • Specific rules appear before general rules.
  • Sensitive segments have explicit access paths.
  • Important events are logged and reviewable.
  • Old policies are reviewed periodically.

Conclusion

A good rule base is more than a list of allows and denies. It is a readable model of trust, access and traffic flow. Keeping that model narrow, documented and intentional improves both security and troubleshooting.
