IP CCTV Troubleshooting Checklist: PoE, Network, Camera & NVR

Before replacing a camera or NVR, find the failed layer. A structured path from physical connectivity to application service makes diagnosis faster and more repeatable.

Step 1: Does the camera have power?

Check link state, switch port status and PoE delivery. If the switch does not detect the device, inspect the cable, termination, patch panel, port and the camera itself before changing software settings.

Step 2: Is the camera visible on the network?

Verify IP address, subnet, gateway and ARP. Test from a host in the same segment. Address conflicts can produce intermittent symptoms that are easy to misread.

Step 3: Validate VLAN and switching

If cameras use a dedicated VLAN, confirm the access VLAN on the camera port and the trunk path upstream. The MAC table can show whether the switch actually sees the device and where.

Step 4: Validate the NVR and stream

When ping works but video is missing, check stream settings, codec compatibility, credentials, system time and NVR resources. Not every video issue is a network issue.

Step 5: Troubleshoot remote access separately

If local video works but external viewing fails, inspect VPN, NAT and firewall policy according to the intended architecture. Do not expose additional management ports just to make the symptom disappear.

Record the root cause

After recovery, document the evidence, action, root cause and validation result. Good documentation turns the next incident into a shorter and more predictable operation.
