10 Essential Tips for Standard Network Cabling

July 2025 | Read Time: 5 min

Network cabling is one of the most critical components of any organization's IT infrastructure. A proper cabling system ensures speed, stability, and significantly lowers maintenance costs. In this article by Mohammad Hossein Asgari Somarin, a Network & Infrastructure Specialist, we explore 10 golden tips for standard network cabling.

1. Choose the Right Cable (Cat6 vs. Fiber Optic)

For small to medium offices, Cat6 is an excellent choice supporting 1 Gbps up to 100 meters. However, for high bandwidth and long distances, Fiber Optic is superior. Assess your needs before purchasing.

2. Follow TIA/EIA-568 Standards

This standard defines the pinout for RJ45 connectors. You must consistently use either T568A or T568B throughout the entire project to avoid crossover issues.

3. Maintain Proper Distance from Noise Sources

Keep network cables at least 30 cm away from power lines and 15 cm from fluorescent lights to prevent Electromagnetic Interference (EMI).

4. Use Patch Panels and Standard Racks

All cables should terminate at patch panels installed in 19-inch racks. This simplifies management, troubleshooting, and future modifications.

5. Label Everything Accurately

Label both ends of every cable with heat-resistant and moisture-resistant labels. This will save you hours of troubleshooting time later.

6. Respect the Bend Radius

The bend radius for Cat6 cables should not be less than 4 times the cable diameter. Excessive bending causes signal loss (attenuation) and increased errors.

7. Test with a Professional Fluke Tester

After installation, test all links with a professional tester (like Fluke) for signal loss, noise, and compliance with industry standards.

8. Use Cable Trays and Conduits

Protect cables and organize pathways using cable trays, ducts, and conduits. This prevents physical damage and maintains aesthetic order.

9. Respect Maximum Length Limits

The maximum length for Cat6 copper cabling is 100 meters (90m solid + 10m patch cord). For longer distances, use fiber optics or signal repeaters.

10. Document the Project

Save cable maps, patch panel layouts, port numbering, and test results along with photos. This documentation is invaluable for future maintenance and support.
